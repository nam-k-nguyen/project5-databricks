Based on the analysis of COVID-19 data for September and October 2021, there is no strong evidence of a consistent negative correlation between vaccination rates and new COVID-19 cases. In fact, the computed Pearson correlation coefficient between people_fully_vaccinated_per_hundred and new_cases_per_million was approximately +0.35, indicating a moderate positive correlation. This result may reflect factors such as delayed vaccine effects, increased testing/reporting in highly vaccinated countries, or the spread of variants like Delta during that period. On the other hand, preliminary observations from average excess_mortality suggest a potential reduction in death rates among countries with higher vaccination levels, although further statistical modeling (e.g., regression) would be required to confirm this trend. Visualizations supported these patterns, showing regional differences and spikes despite high vaccine coverage in some areas. Overall, while vaccination is a key public health measure, the data highlights the complexity of pandemic dynamics and the importance of multi-factor analysis.
